### Notable Gaps

1. **Growth Feeling (Feature #4):** While customer quality bias and inventory upgrades exist, there is no explicit "fame" or "pawn shop reputation tier" progression visible to the player. The design envisions a journey from "struggling to survive" to "famous pawn shop," but this arc is only implicitly conveyed through increasing reputation numbers rather than a dedicated progression system with milestones/unlocks that mark the shop's growing status.

2. **Mother Death Emotional Differentiation (Feature #11):** The GameOverScreen is a generic "LIQUIDATED" display regardless of cause. When the mother dies, the design calls for an emotionally impactful, differentiated experience — not just a standard Game Over. The current screen shows "Deceased" status universally and uses the same skull/red theme for bankruptcy, eviction, and mother's death alike.

3. **Decision Layering - Small Decisions (Feature #18):** The design specifies "slider fine-tuning" and "light bargaining" as a category of small decisions. While the codebase has range slider CSS styling (index.html), the actual negotiation UI uses typed/button-based input rather than a slider for fine-tuning offer amounts. This is a minor UI/UX gap rather than a functional one.

4. **NPC Survival Formula Visibility (Feature #23):** The "Remaining days = funds / dailyCost" formula operates behind the scenes in mail tone selection and urgency determination, but is not surfaced to the player as a visible, understandable mechanic. The player cannot directly see how their pawn amount translates to NPC survival days.

5. **Growth Arc Visibility (Feature #4):** No explicit milestones or visual indicators show the player progressing from "struggling newcomer" to "established pawn shop" beyond raw reputation numbers. Reputation milestones exist in data (assets/data/texts/milestones.csv) but their visibility as a growth narrative is limited.
